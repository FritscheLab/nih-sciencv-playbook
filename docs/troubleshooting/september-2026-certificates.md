# September 2026: re-certify older SciENcv PDFs

{: .warning }
> **Institutional operational alert, reviewed October 2, 2026:** [UCLA's September 25 notice](https://ocga.research.ucla.edu/announcement/ocga-announcement/update-sciencv-documents-for-federal-proposals-submitted-on-or-after-september-26-2026/) reports that SciENcv began using a new PDF signing certificate on **September 19, 2026**, and the previous certificate expired on **September 26, 2026**. PDFs certified **before September 19** may fail validation in applications submitted **on or after September 26**, even when their individual certification is less than 12 months old. The trigger is the **actual submission date**, not the application due date.

[WashU's September 24 alert](https://research.washu.edu/announcements/urgent-biosketch-other-support-notice/) also identifies applications and RPPRs submitted through eRA Commons. As of this review, we have not located a public NIH/eRA/NCBI notice confirming the rollover dates. These are institutional alerts, not a newly established NIH policy deadline; check current agency instructions and your sponsored programs office for updates.

## Before submitting an affected document

1. Review the certification dates of required biosketch and CPOS PDFs already attached to the submission. Flag PDFs certified before September 19, 2026, or whose signing certificate is uncertain.
2. Have the **named individual** open each affected document in SciENcv, click **Download PDF**, review the statement, and click **Certify**. A delegate cannot certify.
3. Download the newly certified PDF and **replace the old attachment** in the application or report. Preserve the generated PDF without editing or flattening, except where NIH expressly requires a special attachment.
4. Re-run the submission system's validations and resolve any remaining errors before submission.

Request CPOS only when it is required for the person's role and submission stage. See the [submission lifecycle]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link other-support/lifecycle-submission.md %}).

For a remaining signature error, retain the original generated file and the validation message, and contact your sponsored programs office or the [eRA Service Desk](https://www.era.nih.gov/need-help). An Adobe Acrobat signature warning alone is not proof of an eRA submission failure; [NIH's Common Forms FAQ](https://grants.nih.gov/faqs#/common-forms-biographical-sketch-current-pending-support.htm?anchor=57855) distinguishes that viewer warning from submission validation.
