# September 2026: re-certify older SciENcv PDFs

{: .warning }
> **Institutional operational alert.** UCLA reports that SciENcv began using a new PDF signing certificate on **September 19, 2026**, and the previous certificate expired on **September 26, 2026**. PDFs certified **before September 19** may fail validation in applications submitted **on or after September 26**, even when the individual certified less than 12 months earlier. Use the date you actually submit to check whether the alert applies. [UCLA's September 25 notice](https://ocga.research.ucla.edu/announcement/ocga-announcement/update-sciencv-documents-for-federal-proposals-submitted-on-or-after-september-26-2026/).

WashU’s alert covers both applications and RPPRs submitted through eRA Commons. As of **November 19, 2026**, the rollover dates remain sourced to institutional alerts. Check current agency instructions and your sponsored programs office for updates. [WashU's September 24 alert](https://research.washu.edu/announcements/urgent-biosketch-other-support-notice/).

## Before submitting an affected document

1. Review the certification dates of required biosketch and CPOS PDFs already attached to the submission. Flag PDFs certified before September 19, 2026, or whose signing certificate is uncertain.
2. Have the **named individual** sign in using their own account, open each affected document in SciENcv, select **Download PDF**, review the statement, and select **Certify**. [My NCBI Help](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK154494/).
3. Use the newly downloaded PDF to **replace the old attachment** in the application or report. Preserve the generated PDF without editing or flattening, except where NIH expressly requires a special attachment.
4. Re-run the submission system's validations and resolve any remaining errors before submission.

Request CPOS only when it is required for the person's role and submission stage. See the [submission lifecycle]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link other-support/lifecycle-submission.md %}).

For a remaining signature error, retain the original generated file and the validation message, and contact your sponsored programs office or the [eRA Service Desk](https://www.era.nih.gov/need-help). An Adobe Acrobat signature warning alone is not proof of an eRA submission failure; [NIH's Common Forms FAQ](https://grants.nih.gov/faqs#/common-forms-biographical-sketch-current-pending-support.htm?anchor=57855) distinguishes that viewer warning from submission validation.
